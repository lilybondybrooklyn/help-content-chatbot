Product discovery · CFPB complaint data × public help content

Help Gap Finder

Which problems do Bank of America customers take to the federal regulator that the bank's own help center doesn't answer? I grouped CFPB complaints (Sept 2024 to Sept 2026) into 24 customer problems, read them against about 390 public help Q&As and articles, and ranked the gaps. The result is a short list of what a self-service chatbot should cover first.

03 · So here's what the chatbot should cover first

Five intents, picked for volume, gap size, growth, and how well a bot can actually help

Ranking isn't only by gap size. Collections is a large gap, but complaints about it are falling, the bank is rarely found at fault (3 to 6% relief), and the topic is legally sensitive. So that one is a content fix first, with the bot routing to a person.

Write the content before building the bot: collections

2,813 complaints and no help content at all. Publish a page on how Bank of America's recovery process works, how to verify or dispute a debt, and how the account is reported to credit bureaus. The bot can then point to it and route to a person.

Quick content fixes

Missing pages for why can't I open an account (886) and how to fix what you reported to my credit bureau (814). Also correct the overdraft page's structured data (section 04).

04 · Two things I found along the way

Findings that matter for anyone building a help chatbot

● Stale content

The overdraft page tells machines one fee and people another

The visible answer to "I thought I had enough money; why did you charge me an overdraft fee?" gives a $10 fee in its example. The page's hidden FAQ structured data (application/ld+json), which search engines and AI answer engines read, still says $35. A retrieval-based chatbot that indexes structured data would quote the old fee.

Lesson for the build: index the visible text, and add a check that compares numbers between the two versions. I ran that check on 76 matched Q&As: this was the only fee mismatch, plus one small wording difference ("14 days" versus "14 business days").

● Data constraint

The complaint stories are gone

On Aug 14, 2026, the CFPB stopped publishing complaint narratives (customers' written descriptions). Older narratives are no longer in the public API either. So this analysis uses the CFPB's structured categories (product, issue, sub-issue) and the company-response field, not text clustering.

What that means: the problem groupings are coarser than clustering the text would give, and there are no quotes from customers. The next version could cluster a bank's own chat transcripts or call reasons instead.

05 · Method and limits

How this was built

Complaints

All CFPB complaints against Bank of America, N.A., received 1 Sept 2024 to 23 Sept 2026, in four products: checking/savings, credit card, debt collection, and money transfer. Each product → issue → sub-issue combination (140 in total) was mapped by ordered rules to one of 24 problems, each worded as the question a customer would ask.

Help content

I crawled 41 public Bank of America help and FAQ pages in the product areas above (deposits, debit, credit card, Zelle, online banking, security center, disputes, credit-card assistance) and extracted each Q&A from the page and its structured data. Pages behind login were out of scope, and so were Erica answers.

Coverage call

For each problem I pulled the closest matches (TF-IDF plus a keyword sweep), read them, and rated the problem covered, partial (explains how to start but not what the complaints are about), or missing. Unanswered complaints = complaints × 1 for missing, × 0.5 for partial. This is a judgment call, and each rating's evidence is in its row above.

Limits: CFPB complaints are a biased sample. They skew toward escalated, unresolved problems and don't represent all customer contacts. Relief rates come from the bank's own response codes. The recent-trend window includes complaints still in progress. The customer-facing timelines in section 03 are the federal maximums (Reg E, Reg Z), not Bank of America policy.
