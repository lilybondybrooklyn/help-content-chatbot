Does the bot know when not to answer?

The questions come from the 24 customer problems in the Help Gap Finder, plus everyday lookups and two off-topic questions. Each one has an expected outcome: answered (the help content covers it), partial (it covers part of it), or handoff (it doesn't, so the bot should decline to guess). A run asks Claude two questions per test, one after another, and takes about 3 minutes. Keep this page open until it finishes. It saves after every question, and you can resume an interrupted run.

Pipeline

Four steps per question

  1. Rewrite. A quick Claude call turns the question into three search queries in the words a help page would use. It also flags off-topic questions, such as investing or mortgage rates.
  2. Retrieve. Keyword search (BM25) runs each query over 548 passages. The results are merged with reciprocal-rank fusion and the top 8 are kept. This all runs in your browser.
  3. Answer. Claude gets only those 8 passages, with instructions to answer from them, cite each claim, and label the result ANSWERED, PARTIAL or HANDOFF. It must not add fees, timelines or phone numbers the passages don't state.
  4. Show your work. The trace panel lists the queries, the retrieved passages and which passages the answer cited, so any answer can be checked.

Why keyword search instead of embeddings

This page runs as a self-contained web page, which can't download an embedding model. Keyword search plus LLM query rewriting works well for a few hundred FAQ passages. The eval measures this directly: on raw questions, keyword search alone finds the right topic in the top 8 for 27 of 29 answerable test questions, and the rewrite step targets the misses. Swapping in embeddings is the next upgrade.

Corpus hygiene

Passages come from the text visible on each page. Hidden structured data (JSON-LD) is used only for questions that don't appear visibly. This matters: Bank of America's overdraft FAQ shows a $10 fee on the page, but its structured data still says $35. Navigation text, state pickers and legal boilerplate are stripped out, duplicate answers are merged, and long answers are split into passages of about 900 characters.

Built from the gap analysis

The test questions come from 30,763 CFPB complaints grouped into customer problems. The gap analysis showed which problems the help content covers, so the eval can check that the bot answers those, gives partial answers where coverage is partial, and hands off where it's missing, instead of inventing an answer.
